This is the authoritative hidden-discovery protocol for Section~\ref{sec:hidden-slice-method}; Appendix~\ref{app:discovery-results} reports outcomes. A frozen per-example gate transfers to evaluation data, where normalization and declared support correction are recomputed. \subsection{Gate Family and Support Constraints}
\label{app:gate-support}
For frozen standardized features $\psi(x)$ and task class $c$, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\phi,c}(x)&=\sigma(a_c^\top\psi(x)+b_c),\qquad
q_i^0=\frac{h_{\phi,c}(x_i)}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal D_c}h_{\phi,c}(x_j)},\quad
\sigma(t)=(1+e^{-t})^{-1},\\
m_{\mathrm{nom}}&=\tau_{\mathrm{slice}}n_c,\qquad
m_{\mathrm{ESS}}=\min\{n_c,\max\{1,0.8m_{\mathrm{nom}}\}\},\qquad
b_{\max}=\min\{1,2/m_{\mathrm{nom}}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $n_c=|\mathcal D_c|$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{slice}}=0.10$ is within-class mass. Discovery calibrates $b_c$ so that mean membership equals $\tau_{\mathrm{slice}}$; the selected checkpoint's intercept is frozen at evaluation, without score re-centering or mass recalibration. The cap denominator is nominal slice size, not the ESS floor.

Support correction projects $q^0$ onto the capped simplex only if its maximum exceeds $b_{\max}$. Writing the result as $q^{\mathrm{cap}}$ and $u_i=1/n_c$, it takes $\widetilde q=u+t(q^{\mathrm{cap}}-u)$ with the largest $t\in[0,1]$ satisfying $\sum_i\widetilde q_i^2\le1/m_{\mathrm{ESS}}$. Thus $\widetilde q\ge0$, $\sum_i\widetilde q_i=1$, $\max_i\widetilde q_i\le b_{\max}$, and $n_{\eff}=(\sum_i\widetilde q_i^2)^{-1}\ge m_{\mathrm{ESS}}$. These constraints do not imply nuisance conditioning. Both discovery and evaluation use their own class sample size, so $\widetilde q$ depends on the evaluation batch. Reported mass is mean \emph{raw} membership within the selected class; ESS uses $\widetilde q$.

\paragraph{Metrics and overlap.}
\label{app:reported-slice-metrics}
Accuracy, uncalibrated binary NLL (temperature one), and post-hoc group composition are averages under $\widetilde q$, without information pooling; predicted labels threshold the head logit at zero. Calibrated NLL is stored separately. Fisher and high-loss gates share these conventions. Random controls weight $k=\lceil\tau_{\mathrm{slice}}n_c\rceil$ sampled rows of the Fisher-selected class uniformly; the constant gate uses $1/n_c$. Unweighted and centered-unweighted controls reevaluate the predictive-Fisher and high-loss gates without rediscovery.

Jaccard overlap uses the $k=\max\{1,\lceil\tau_{\mathrm{slice}}n_c\rceil\}$ largest raw memberships in each selected class, intersected on original row identities; different classes give zero. All ten archived Fisher/high-loss cutoffs are untied. Fold stability applies the same top-count rule on common same-class training-calibration reference rows, averaging fold-pair overlaps within each class, then weighting class averages by selected-fold counts; classes with fewer than two selected gates are excluded. Neither comparison uses positive-membership supports or corrected-weight ranks.

\subsection{Discovery Objective and Settings}
\label{app:discovery-objective}
With the declared matrix $M_i$ and same-class pool $\widehat J_{\mathrm{pool},c}=n_c^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal D_c}M_i$, search each class and select its best feasible checkpoint/restart by discovery utility:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat J_{\phi,c}^{(\eta)}&=(1-\eta)\sum_i\widetilde q_i(\phi)M_i+\eta\widehat J_{\mathrm{pool},c},\\
(c^\star,\phi^\star)&\in\argmax_{c,\phi}
\left\{\lambda_{\min}\!\left(S_{\lambda_{\rid}}(\widehat J_{\phi,c}^{(\eta)})\right)+\varepsilon\right\}^{-1},
\qquad 0\le\eta<1.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:hidden-slice-discovery}
\end{equation}
The operational task/nuisance blocks are scalar. The optimizer uses $-\log(\widehat F^{\rid}+\varepsilon)$ minus ten times squared relative mass error; the log preserves ranking at fixed mass. The intercept is recalibrated while AdamW updates gate weights, and a constant gate is an eligible baseline. High-loss search uses the same family, mass, and support rules with weighted prediction-loss utility.

\subsection{Held-Out Targets and Uncertainty}
\label{app:heldout-ratio}
Condition on all fitting data/objects and a selected-class distribution $P_c$. For its frozen gate $h$ and matrix map $M$, the unshrunk target and empirical ratio are
\begin{equation}
J_h=\frac{A}{p},\quad A=\E_{P_c}[hM],\quad p=\E_{P_c}[h]>0;
\qquad \widehat J_h=\frac{\widehat A}{\widehat p},\quad
(\widehat A,\widehat p)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(h_iM_i,h_i).
\label{eq:hidden-slice-population-target}
\end{equation}
\begin{appendixproposition}[Frozen-gate ratio bound]
\label{prop:frozen-gate-ratio}
For i.i.d. evaluation observations from $P_c$, assume $0\le h\le1$ and $0\preceq M\preceq M_{\max}I_d$, with finite $M_{\max}$ specified independently of evaluation data. Given $0<\delta<1$, let
\[
u=\log(4d/\delta),\qquad
r_A=M_{\max}\left(\sqrt{2u/n}+2u/(3n)\right),\qquad
r_p=\sqrt{\log(4/\delta)/(2n)}.
\]
With conditional probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously $\lVert\widehat A-A\rVert_{\op}\le r_A$ and $|\widehat p-p|\le r_p$. Whenever $\underline p=\widehat p-r_p>0$ on this event,
\begin{equation}
\lVert\widehat J_h-J_h\rVert_{\op}
\le r_J:=\frac{r_A+\lVert\widehat J_h\rVert_{\op}r_p}{\widehat p-r_p}.
\label{eq:frozen-gate-ratio-radius}
\end{equation}
\end{appendixproposition}
\begin{proof}
The centered matrices $h_iM_i-A$ have norm at most $M_{\max}$ and variance bounded by $M_{\max}^2I_d$. Matrix Bernstein~\citep{tropp2012user} gives the numerator event with failure at most $\delta/2$; Hoeffding gives the denominator event with failure at most $\delta/2$. Their union bound and
\[
\widehat J_h-J_h=
\frac{(\widehat A-A)-\widehat J_h(\widehat p-p)}{p},
\qquad p\ge\widehat p-r_p>0,
\]
prove the observable bound. The shared random denominator is therefore explicitly controlled.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Pooling and support correction.}
Set $J_{\mathrm{pool},c}=\E_{P_c}[M]$ and $J_h^{(\eta)}=(1-\eta)J_h+\eta J_{\mathrm{pool},c}$. Allocate $\delta_{\mathrm{ratio}}+\delta_{\mathrm{pool}}=\delta$, using the former in Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen-gate-ratio}. For the evaluation mean $\widehat J_{\mathrm{pool},c}=n^{-1}\sum_iM_i$, matrix Bernstein supplies
\[
r_{\mathrm{pool}}=M_{\max}\left[\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2d/\delta_{\mathrm{pool}})}n}
+\frac{2\log(2d/\delta_{\mathrm{pool}})}{3n}\right].
\]
Writing $\widetilde J_h=\sum_i\widetilde q_iM_i$, the triangle inequality gives
$\lVert\widetilde J_h-\widehat J_h\rVert_{\op}\le M_{\max}\sum_i|\widetilde q_i-q_i^0|=:r_{\mathrm{proj}}$.
Hence, on the simultaneous event and when $\underline p>0$,
\begin{equation}
\lVert(1-\eta)\widetilde J_h+\eta\widehat J_{\mathrm{pool},c}-J_h^{(\eta)}\rVert_{\op}
\le(1-\eta)(r_J+r_{\mathrm{proj}})+\eta r_{\mathrm{pool}}.
\label{eq:gate-projection-allowance}
\end{equation}
The union bound permits dependence between pool and ratio estimates; $r_{\mathrm{proj}}$ covers batch-dependent support correction. If the pool is fixed from fitting data, it belongs to the conditional target and needs no evaluation-error term. Appendix~\ref{app:lower-audit} converts the matrix allowance into a Schur/eigenvalue lower certificate, requiring positive lower membership mass and nuisance-conditioning margins. The target retains shrinkage; evaluation-estimated centering additionally requires first-moment control.

\paragraph{Reported uncertainty.}
Each of 2,000 within-selected-class bootstrap replicates resamples aligned membership, task, nuisance, logit, and label rows, recomputing normalized/support-corrected weights and the same-class pool. Gate coefficients/intercept, coordinate normalizers, and temperature stay frozen. The 95\% interval is the 2.5th/97.5th percentile of predictive projected information (linear quantile interpolation). Across five paired seeds, form $\Delta_s=T_s^{\mathrm{gate}}-T_s^{\mathrm{control}}$ first, then use their sample standard deviation $s_\Delta$ to report $\overline\Delta\pm t_{0.975,4}s_\Delta/\sqrt5$; single-procedure intervals use the same rule on its five values. Bootstrap uncertainty is conditional on fitted objects; seed intervals reflect fitting variation on the shared benchmark. Neither is a computation or validation of the finite-sample radii above.
